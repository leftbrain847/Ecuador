Which bike route · Option A

Route 1, and the reason is skill, not altitude. By the 19th we are well acclimatised and that argument is settled. What acclimatisation does not do is teach you to ride loose volcanic scree. Between us: Papa Bear rides city roads in traffic weekly, Mama Bear less, and neither of us has ridden off road. Loose steep surfaces punish exactly the instincts road riding builds — braking hard, weight forward — and Route 2 opens with switchbacks and drops on ash and scree at 4,800 m, an hour from tarmac, with the support vehicle on the road rather than the trail. It is not a question of being fit or being game.

Route 1 is not a consolation prize. Its first 8 km are dirt switchbacks from 4,850 m — real off-road descending, and the part of the day worth having. What we give up is 20 km of singletrack we do not have the hands for yet.

If we want to change this, the deadline is at home, not on the mountain. Two weeks before departure is not enough to build technical skill, but it is enough to ride some loose gravel and find out honestly where we stand. If that goes well, ask the operator about switching — Andean Trails’ write-up notes the sandy section has a road bail-out, so the routes are not fully separate. Decide from evidence, not from how we feel at the trailhead.

Road condition, either way. The paved 29 km runs on the Calpi–San Juan–El Arenal highway, in poor condition for years — local reporting counted more than 200 potholes on the Calpi–San Juan stretch. Resurfacing was announced in May 2026 with partial lane closures between 08:00 and 17:00, and further asphalt repairs were posted in July 2026. Descending 29 km in a coned-off lane beside interprovincial buses is a different activity from the one the brochures sell. Ask what it looks like now; the shorter dirt-only version with a van pickup is a fine outcome.

The Black Sheep Inn — Eco-lodge above the Toachi canyon. Sauna, hot tub, yoga room, zipline, wood stoves, three meals in. The most comfortable bed on the Loop. Priciest; vegetarian-only; composting toilets.

Hostal Doña Clarita — Village homestay, $15pp including breakfast, dinner and unlimited tea. The most authentic and the most basic. Thin information available.

17 SepQuilotoa village · 3,900 mBook now

Highest, coldest, and by common agreement the weakest lodging on the Loop — guides say the hostels here disappoint after Isinliví and Chugchilán. We are here for sunrise, not the room. Confirm heating or blanket count in writing.

No ATM in the village. Bring the cash or check what they take.

Four worth considering, plus overflow

Runa Wasi Quilotoa — Five minutes’ walk from the rim, wood-burning stove in the room, private bathroom, hot water. Takes PayPal for a $2 surcharge, which matters where there is no ATM. Private rooms around $56. Food and hospitality rated only fair.

Hostería Alpaka Quilotoa — On the main road, 550 m from the viewpoint — closest of the four. Dorm beds from about $15, restaurant, luggage storage, and they arrange guides, transport and horses.

Hostería Chukirawa — Opposite the information office, 19 rooms, breakfast and dinner included, around $44 a double. The largest place in the village, so most likely to have space late.

Hotel Princesa Toa — The highest standard in the village by some distance, and priced to match — around $100 a double.

Overflow — Samay Kirutoa Lodge, Cabañas Quilotoa, Shalala Quilotoa and Martita’s House all exist if the above are full.

Before booking any of these

Prices above come from reviews and listings spanning several years and disagree with each other by two to three times in places. Treat every figure as indicative and confirm by email or WhatsApp.

Nearly everything on the Loop includes breakfast and dinner, because there is almost nowhere else to eat. Confirm half board is in the rate rather than assuming it.

Most of these are cash only and there is no ATM past Latacunga.

How the day runs

Composite of what Riobamba operators publish. Confirm the exact version when we book.

08:00–08:30 · Hotel pickup Van from Riobamba, about an hour to the reserve. Register at the control point on the way in — everyone entering has to.
~10:00 · Carrel refuge, 4,850 m Drive right to the parking area. Toilets and a café here. This is where the bikes and the support vehicle wait all day.
10:30 · Walk to Whymper, 5,030–5,060 m About 1 km on a marked stony path, 45 minutes to an hour. Taxing at that altitude, not technical, no gear beyond warm layers.
Optional · Condor Cocha, ~5,100 m Ten minutes further on the short version, or 1.5–2 hours for the longer loop. Decide this at Whymper based on how we're actually doing, not at breakfast.
~13:00 · Back at Carrel, gear up Helmet, elbow and knee pads, safety briefing and a test ride on the flat before anyone commits to the descent.
13:30 · Descend to San Juan 8 km dirt switchbacks then paved road, 4,850 m down to roughly 2,900–3,200 m. No pedalling required. Support vehicle stays with the group.
~14:30 · Lunch in San Juan, van back Back in Riobamba around 15:00. Bikes go home in the van; nothing for us to return.

Go / no-go

Run this twice: the evening of the 18th, and again at the trailhead before we start walking — Carrel refuge under A, the 4,600 m parking area under C. Either of us can call it off for both of us, no negotiation.

Headache that doesn't clear with water and rest Don't go up. This is the one symptom that reliably precedes the bad version. Stand down
Nausea, dizziness, or a bad night's sleep with breathlessness Stand down and re-roll tomorrow. Riobamba is a fine place to lose a day. Stand down
Symptoms appear on the walk above the parking area Turn around immediately and descend to the van. Descent is the treatment; there is no shelter to wait it out in up there. Turn around
Both of us clear, weather clear Walk to Whymper. Reassess there before adding Condor Cocha — the extension is optional and the mountain is not going anywhere. Go
One of us clear, one not The one who's fine can still do the day; the one who isn't stays low. Don't split by pushing someone up the hill. Split

We arrive at this day well prepared, and the plan should say so. By the morning of the 19th we will have slept six nights above 2,400 m, three at or above 3,200 m, one at 3,900 m, with a rest day built in at Chugchilán. That is real preexposure, not a cold start, and the expectation is that both of us are fine.

The rules above are not a prediction, they are a pre-commitment. 5,100 m is still about 1,200 m higher than we will ever have slept, and acclimatisation protects roughly to where you have been. The point of deciding this in a hotel room on the 18th is that at 5,000 m, with the tour paid for and the guide waiting, nobody makes this call well.

One real difference: A has a re-roll day on the 20th and C does not. Under C, if the 19th goes badly we are on the bus to Baños regardless, which is the right treatment anyway.

Ask the operators

Contact two or three, compare the answers, then book. Julio Verne Travel runs the classic Riobamba hike-and-bike with a beginner and an advanced route; Chimborazo Tours Ecuador and Ecuador Adventures Trip both publish day tours around $65 per person with a two-person minimum.

01Confirm in writing we are on Route 1, the dirt-and-paved route. Also ask: is the opening 8 km off Carrel the same on both routes, and can we switch on the day if the first section goes well?
02What condition is the paved section to San Juan in right now, given the resurfacing works? Is there a shorter dirt-only option with a van pickup?
03Support vehicle behind the group the whole way, or meeting us at points?
04Helmet, elbow and knee pads included? Gloves? Any of it optional?
05Guide speaks English? Has the guide turned a group around for altitude before, and what's their threshold?
06If one of us stops at Whymper and one continues, how does that work with a single guide?
07Is the Carrel road open and normal this week? Any reserve restrictions?
08If we cancel the morning of for weather or altitude, what happens to the money and can we re-book for the next day?
09Do you collect from Baños, or from Chimborazo Lodge / Casa Cóndor, and what does that cost?

Options by effort

Everything below the summit is walking. No technical gear anywhere on this list except the last line.

4,850 m Drive to Carrel refuge No walking beyond the parking area. Paved road, no 4x4 needed. Toilets and a café.
5,050 m Carrel → Whymper refuge About an hour, roughly 200 m of gain on a marked path. Taxing at that altitude, not technical.
~5,100 m On to Condor Cocha lagoon Ten minutes beyond Whymper on the short version, or 1.5–2 hours for the longer one.
~5,250 m Whymper → Chamonix Needles Around 2 hours beyond Whymper. Scree and rock, no scrambling. Operators want helmet and poles, and good acclimatisation. Guided · 20 Sep only
Full day Needles + descent to La Chorrera The above, then 3–4 hours descending to about 3,900 m and a pickup. The best walking day on this mountain. Guided
37 km Downhill bike — Route 1 About 8 km of dirt switchbacks off Carrel, then 29 km paved to San Juan. Note the first 8 km is genuine off-road descending from 4,850 m — this is not a paved-only day, and it is where the ride actually is. Support vehicle follows, briefing and test ride first. Our route · around $65–70 pp
34 km Downhill bike — Route 2 34 km, roughly 20 km off road: singletrack, steeper descents, a sand-and-gravel mix and a few uphills at over 4,000 m. Operators describe the opening as rocky trail turning to loose volcanic ash and scree with sharp switchbacks and steep drops, and list body armour as essential. Rated for experienced mountain bikers. Skill, not nerve · see below
6,263 m Summit Two days, glacier route, crampons and ice axe, overnight start. Operators want a week of Andean acclimatisation first. Not compatible with this trip. Guide mandatory

Costs & access

Option A. Confirm all three with a Riobamba operator before the 19th.

Reserve entry Older operator pages quote about $6 for foreigners and $2 for Ecuadorians. Ecuadorian reporting in 2025 said reserve access is officially free for nationals and foreigners alike. Small money either way — carry small bills and don't be surprised by either answer.
Whymper refuge Reported closed and derelict as accommodation, with water and infrastructure problems, though tours still stop there. Treat it as a waypoint with a toilet, not shelter and not a bail-out point if the weather turns.
Getting up there independently Taxi from Riobamba to the parking area around $35 round trip, or hourly Guaranda-bound buses will drop you at the signed turnoff. Fine for the walk; the bike day needs an operator.
